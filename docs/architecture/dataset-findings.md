# What the seed profile actually tells us

The machine measures ([`dataset-profile.md`](dataset-profile.md), regenerated by
`scripts/profile_seed.py`); this document interprets. Keeping them separate matters: measurements
are facts that can be re-derived, interpretations are judgements that can be wrong and must be
attributable.

Every finding below changed a decision. Findings that changed nothing are not recorded — a
profile report nobody acts on is a ritual.

---

## F1 — The skew is real, so the amplifier does not need to fake it

**Measured.** In 2.6M transaction lines across 582 stores:

| | Top 1% of keys | Top 10% of keys | max / median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `STORE_ID` | 12.2% of lines | **69.3%** of lines | **2,519×** |
| `PRODUCT_ID` | 35.2% of lines | 77.1% of lines | **9,926×** |
| `household_key` | 5.2% of lines | 32.2% of lines | 9× |

**Interpretation.** This is a textbook power law, and it is *native to grocery retail* — flagship
stores and staple SKUs dominate. The `PRODUCT_ID` skew is worse than the store skew, which is the
opposite of what most people assume when they reach for salting.

**What changed.** [ADR-0003](../adr/ADR-0003-dataset-selection.md) originally listed store skew as
a scenario the synthetic amplifier would *inject*. That is now unnecessary and, worse, would have
been misleading — an induced skew is a strawman, and a reviewer is entitled to ask whether the
mitigation would work on real distributions. It works on these because these are real.

The amplifier's `skew.store_concentration` knob is retained but repurposed: it can *amplify* the
existing skew to stress-test beyond the observed level, not manufacture it. The default is now
"preserve observed", not "inject".

**Consequence for the performance lab.** `household_key` at 9× is *not* skewed enough to matter.
Any join keyed on household will not produce a straggler, so the lab must key on `STORE_ID` or
`PRODUCT_ID` to demonstrate anything. Choosing the wrong key would have produced a lab that shows
"AQE fixed it" when AQE was never needed — a false positive that is worse than no lab at all.

## F2 — The wide join silently drops 467 stores, and that is the demo

**Measured.**

- `transaction_data` covers **582** stores.
- `causal_data` (promotion exposure: display, mailer) covers **115** stores — 19.8% of them.
- But those 115 stores carry **98.6%** of transaction lines.

**Interpretation.** dunnhumby collected in-store promotion data for the large stores only. The 467
absent stores are the long tail — individually negligible, collectively 1.4% of volume and **80% of
the store count**.

**Why this is the single best teaching artifact in the dataset.** An inner join of transactions to
promotion exposure loses 1.4% of revenue — which nobody notices — while losing 80% of stores, which
destroys any store-count metric. A dashboard reading "stores with sales: 115" would be wrong by
5×, and the revenue number sitting next to it would look fine.

This is the failure mode that survives code review, survives testing against row counts, and gets
caught in a meeting nine months later by someone who knows the business. It is exactly what
requirement **QLT-004** (referential integrity) and **MOD-003** (join cardinality) exist to catch.

**What changed.** The gold layer now carries an explicit `promo_coverage` flag rather than
inner-joining, and `ops.join_coverage` records match rates per join per run. A join whose match
rate moves is a schema or business change, and it should page someone.

## F3 — Promotion history does not cover the full transaction window

**Measured.** `causal_data.WEEK_NO` spans 9–101. `transaction_data.WEEK_NO` spans 1–102.

**Interpretation.** The first 8 weeks and the final week have transactions but no promotion
exposure. Any feature of the form "was this product on display when purchased?" is *undefined*,
not *false*, for those periods.

**What changed.** Feature engineering must encode this as a three-state value (`on_display`,
`not_on_display`, `unknown`), never as a boolean defaulted to false. Defaulting to false would
teach the model that the first 8 weeks had no promotions, which is a fabricated signal — the same
class of error as the calendar-anchor problem in ADR-0003. Requirement **MLR-006** covers it.

The training window is therefore weeks 9–101, not 1–102. Stating this up front prevents the
"why does the model do badly at the start of the series?" investigation that would otherwise
consume a day.

## F4 — Only a third of households have demographics

**Measured.** 2,500 households transact; 801 (32%) have demographic attributes.

**Interpretation.** Demographics cannot be a required feature. A model trained on the 801 would be
trained on a self-selected third — households that completed a loyalty profile — and its
performance would not transfer to the other 1,699.

**What changed.** The churn model (D2) is built on **behavioural** features (recency, frequency,
monetary, basket composition, category entropy, promo responsiveness), all derivable for 100% of
households. Demographics enter only as an optional enrichment, and the model is evaluated **both**
with and without them so the lift attributable to demographics is measurable rather than assumed.

## F5 — `BRAND` has two values, and it is not what the name says

**Measured.** `product.BRAND` has cardinality **2**.

**Interpretation.** It is `National` vs `Private` label — a *tier* flag, not a brand name. The
actual brand-ish dimension is `MANUFACTURER` (6,476 distinct).

**What changed.** The column is renamed `brand_tier` in silver. A gold metric named "sales by
brand" fed by this column would be silently answering a different question than the one asked, and
the name is the whole reason. Requirement **GOV-004** (terms resolve to a glossary entry) exists
for this class of error.

This is a small finding with an outsized lesson: the most dangerous columns are not the missing
ones, they are the ones whose name means something other than what it says.

## F6 — Outliers that are not errors

**Measured.** `transaction_data.QUANTITY` ranges 0 to **89,638**. `SALES_VALUE` ranges 0 to 840.

**Interpretation.** A quantity of 89,638 is not a data error — it is a weight-priced item where
quantity is expressed in grams. A `SALES_VALUE` of 0 is not an error either: it is a fully
coupon-offset line.

**What changed.** The naive quality rule (`QUANTITY BETWEEN 1 AND 100`) would quarantine legitimate
revenue. Instead, quality rules are conditioned on the product's selling unit, and zero-value lines
are validated against a matching coupon discount rather than rejected.

This is the argument for **QLT-005** — profiling proposes rules, a human disposes. DQX's profiler
would happily have generated the naive range rule from the interquartile range, and it would have
been wrong in a way that only shows up as a slow revenue leak in the quarantine table.

## F7 — No nulls where you expect them, nulls where you do not

**Measured.** Zero blanks in `transaction_data`. In `product`: `CURR_SIZE_OF_PRODUCT` blank in
**33.1%** of rows, and `DEPARTMENT` / `COMMODITY_DESC` / `SUB_COMMODITY_DESC` blank in 15 rows each
(0.02%).

**Interpretation.** The fact layer is complete; the dimension has real gaps. Those 15 products with
no department are the interesting ones: they will not roll up into any category total, so
department-level sales will silently fail to reconcile with overall sales.

**What changed.** Two things. First, an `UNCATEGORISED` member in the product dimension, so
department roll-ups sum to the grand total by construction rather than by luck — this is standard
dimensional practice and the reason it exists is exactly this. Second, because the fact table has
no natural nulls, the null-handling quality rules cannot be validated by the seed alone; they are
tested against injected cases. A rule that has never seen a violation is an untested rule.

## Net effect on the plan

| Original plan | Revised |
|---|---|
| Inject store skew synthetically | Skew is native; the amplifier preserves it, and can amplify beyond observed for stress tests |
| Amplifier needed to make joins hurt | `causal_data` at 36.8M rows already makes the wide join hurt; amplification is for streaming volume only |
| Quality rules generated by profiling | Generated as *candidates*, human-reviewed (F6 shows why) |
| Demographics as model features | Behavioural features carry the model; demographics measured as optional lift (F4) |
| Promotion exposure as boolean | Three-state, with an explicit training window of weeks 9–101 (F3) |
